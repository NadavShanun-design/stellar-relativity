# FAQ

**Why do travellers age less than people on Earth?**
A moving clock runs slow by the Lorentz factor γ. Onboard (proper) time is the
Earth-frame time divided by γ, so the traveller always ages less. See
[`docs/PHYSICS.md`](PHYSICS.md).

**What's the difference between `travel` and `rocket`?**
`travel` assumes a constant cruise velocity (isolates pure time dilation).
`rocket` models a realistic constant-*g* flip-and-burn with acceleration and
deceleration — slower in ship time than the idealised cruise, but physical.

**Are the distances exact?**
No — they are rounded, measured parallaxes with real-world uncertainty, sourced
from public catalogs. The physics is exact given the catalog values.

**Does it model gravity / general relativity?**
No. Everything here is *special* relativity (flat spacetime). GR effects near
massive bodies are out of scope.

**Why light years and not metres everywhere?**
Light years make the relationships legible: light-travel time in years equals the
distance in light years by definition.
